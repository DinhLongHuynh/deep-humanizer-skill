Recent advances in end-to-end speech translation have demonstrated the potential of direct source-to-target mapping without intermediate text representations. However, these models often suffer from limited training data and computational efficiency challenges. This paper proposes a knowledge distillation framework to improve end-to-end speech translation by leveraging knowledge from larger, well-trained teacher models. We investigate both offline and online distillation strategies, transferring dark knowledge from cascaded speech recognition and machine translation systems as well as from larger end-to-end models. Our approach incorporates attention-based knowledge transfer mechanisms to capture fine-grained alignments between source audio and target text. Experiments on the MuST-C and CoVoST 2 datasets show consistent improvements of 1.5–2.8 BLEU points over baseline models, with reduced model size and faster inference. The student models maintain competitive translation quality while achieving approximately 40% parameter reduction. We further analyze the contribution of different knowledge sources and demonstrate that combining multiple distillation signals yields superior performance. Our findings suggest that knowledge distillation is an effective approach for building practical speech translation systems that balance quality, efficiency, and scalability.
