# Operational signals for measuring the human-AI gap (CPU-oriented)

Companion to `literature/embedding_detection_bibliography.md` (deep-humanizer skill, research track on distribution- and embedding-level detection). Compiled 13 November 2026.

## Reading this table

- **Direction** gives the value typical of instruction-tuned LLM output relative to human reference text in the cited study. "AI higher" means a larger value indicates machine-like text.
- **Reported magnitude** quotes only numbers found in the abstract or the PDF text of the cited paper. "Not extracted" means the paper reports results but no number was pulled for this table. Effect sizes come from the original models and data and **do not transfer** to a different scoring model, register or length. Each signal has to be recalibrated on local human and AI reference texts.
- **Min. length** is what the paper used or reports. Where the value is our own inference, it is marked *(inference)*.
- **Robustness** summarises evidence on paraphrase, humanizers, LLM rewriting and decoding changes, using these sources: RAID (Dugan et al. 2024), DIPPER (Krishna et al. 2023), ARB (2607.29539), Russell et al. 2025, Cheng et al. 2025 (adversarial paraphrasing) and HIP (Xu et al. 2026).
- **CPU** is a qualitative cost based on model size and the number of forward passes. No timings were measured for this table.
- Unless a row states otherwise, a recipe parameter is the published setting. Our proposed substitutions for a CPU toolkit appear only in the recommendations section.

| C1 | **Mixture fraction alpha** (Liang et al., ICML 2024; Nature Human Behaviour 2025; Patterns 2025) | Vocabulary V = all adjectives (adverbs, verbs and non-technical nouns also work). ## What survives paraphrase and humanizers: synthesis

1. **Geometric whole-text properties survive lexical paraphrase.** PHD barely moved under DIPPER (A1). Model fingerprints survive rephrasing and translation (A6). Syntactic templates and POS/dependency distributions are untouched by synonym substitution (D2, D3). None of these has been tested against modern commercial humanizers or against LLM rewriting of AI text.
2. **Token-probability detectors are the most fragile family.** Binoculars and Fast-DetectGPT detect 6.7% and 23.3% of humanized O1-PRO articles (Russell et al.). They miss most LLM-rewritten human text (ARB: 15.1% and 30.8% at 1% FPR). Sampling settings alone (repetition penalty) cost up to 32 points (RAID). A humanizer can therefore "pass" these detectors without changing the deeper distribution. Low B3/B4 scores alone are not evidence of human-likeness.
3. **Plain LLM paraphrase can make text more detectable** for some detectors: TPR at 1% FPR rose by 8.57% for RADAR and 15.03% for Fast-DetectGPT (Cheng et al. 2025). Instruction-tuned rewriting re-imposes instruction-tuning features: Biber differentiators resist prompting (2604.14111), and base-model text reads as human to commercial detectors (HIP). A rewriting engine built on a base model, or on a minimally tuned paraphraser, is better grounded than an instruct model prompted to "sound human".
4. **The gap is in dispersion as much as in means.** Human text has higher variability in surprisal (B5, B6, B7), sentence length (D4), linguistic features (A9) and across documents (A8). Optimise toward the human *distribution* (percentile bands, MMD), not toward a single target mean, which would itself create a new uniformity.
5. **False positives are structural.** Non-native and plain-style human writing scores as AI on likelihood-type signals (B1). FPR depends on length (E3). Reference corpora collected after 2023 are contaminated with LLM text (C1 society-wide estimates). Calibrate on pre-2023 or vetted human text from the same register.

**Pipeline note.** One base-model forward pass (Qwen2.5-1.5B) yields B1, B2, B3, B7 and B8 together. One instruct-model pass adds B4 and A4. One `roberta-base` pass gives A1 and A2, and GPT-2 small gives B6. The rest needs a tagger and a sentence encoder. Per 512-token text this totals about four small-model forward passes, which is compatible with a rewrite-measure-rewrite loop on a 10-core CPU. Runtime was not benchmarked here.

**Suggested reporting in the check round.** For each signal family (geometry, probability, lexical, register), report the candidate text's percentile within the length-binned human reference distribution, before and after rewriting. Add batch-level MMD (A7) and homogeneity (A8) when several texts are processed. Apply hard gates E1 and E2. Report external detectors (open and commercial) separately, and never use them to guide rewriting (E4).
