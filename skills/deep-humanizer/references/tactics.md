## Full tactic map (rewrite operations → target signals → evidence → verification)

Reference file for the `deep-humanizer` skill. Every evidence cell points to an entry in `literature/humanization_research.md`, where identifiers and venues are verified. Values are quoted from the sources; "—" means the source gives no effect size for this operation.

**Signal levels:** **S** surface/lexical · **Syn** syntactic/register · **D** discourse/content · **P** LM-probability · **E** embedding geometry/style.

**How to read "expected effect".** For linguistic tactics, the number is the human–LLM gap the tactic tries to close. It is not a promise that detectors will move. For evasion-derived tactics, it is the detector change reported in the source for the closest operation studied. No source measured these tactics as stand-alone rewrite operations on detectors. Treat them as hypotheses that the skill's CHECK round tests.

**Global rules (from the evidence):**
1. Edit, don't regenerate. A full LLM paraphrase lowers perplexity below human (9.28 vs 15.02; Cheng et al. 2025). It keeps the paraphraser's own fingerprint (Sun et al. 2025) and iterates into attractor cycles (Wang et al. 2025). Writer-style targeted edits are preferred (LAMP: 74% of expert edits are replacements).
2. Aim for the register's human interval, never an extreme. Overshooting perplexity (300–400; AuthorMist) or collapsing length and diversity (SHADE; Karr et al.) is as non-human as the original.
3. Meaning is a hard gate. Use bidirectional NLI plus number/entity preservation; embedding similarity is not enough (StealthRL: E5 0.901 with NLI 0.316).
4. Never add facts. Specificity can only come from the source text or from material the user supplies.
5. Character-level tricks are out of scope (Section C).

## A. Rewrite tactics

| # | Tactic | Target signal(s) | Mechanism | Evidence (source) | Expected effect / gap to close | Risks | How to verify after rewriting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Register-aware removal of excess vocabulary.** Replace or delete words from `ai_vocabulary.json` that are marked for the text's register (e.g. *delve, underscore, intricate, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, camaraderie, meticulous, commendable*). Often the right edit is deletion, because the word is decorative. | S; P (minor) | Removes vocabulary that instruction tuning or preference learning amplified | Kobak 2025 (delves r = 28.0); Juzek & Ward 2025 (delves +6697%); Liang ICML 2024 (meticulous 34.7-fold); Reinhart 2025 (camaraderie 162×, absent from base models); Gray 2024; Matsui 2025 | Removes the lexical marker. Detector effect is small and uneven: RAID synonym swap moved Binoculars −36.1 and GLTR −31.4, but RADAR only −3.4 and Originality +11.5. StoryScope narrative detection stayed at 93.9% macro-F1 after span-level cleanup (−1.6 points). | Replacement by another marked synonym (LLMs spread over synonyms: Suvanto +0.29 bits entropy); register mismatch (these words are normal in fiction: Reinhart); loss of a real technical term | Hits per 1,000 words, weighted by `by_term.n_sources`, before vs after; no new hits introduced; compare with a human reference of the same register |
| T2 | **Remove stock constructions and n-grams.** "It's not X, it's Y"; "In conclusion, …"; "a sense of / the weight of / a mix of"; Antislop trigrams ("voice barely above a whisper", "took a deep breath", "air thick with the scent"). | S; Syn | Breaks high-frequency multiword patterns that recur across models | Antislop 2025 (construction 6.3×; trigrams on 19–69% of 67 models' top lists); Herbold 2023 (identical "In conclusion"); LAMP 2025 ("unspoken" in ~15% of responses; weight of / sense of / mix of rare in human seeds) | Gap: 6.3× for the construction; up to 1,192× for specific trigrams (gemma-3-12b) | Over-correction, e.g. banning legitimate uses. Token banning lowered quality to 28/100 at 8k patterns (Antislop). Rewrite the sentence instead of banning tokens. | Regex and stopword-removed trigram matcher against `ai_vocabulary.json` (unit = trigram, construction) |
| T3 | **Keep lexical diversity inside the register's human band; do not maximise it.** Allow repetition of the key term instead of elegant variation; reduce synonym cycling. | S; P | LLMs either under-shoot (some open models) or over-shoot (GPT-4, fiction rewrites) human diversity. Humanizers flatten it. | Muñoz-Ortiz 2024 (MTLD human 96.51 vs 57.37–94.56); Herbold 2023 (ChatGPT-4 > human); Suvanto 2026 (wider synonym variety); Karr 2026 (humanization lowers diversity); SHADE (lower TTR, hapax) | Gap is model- and register-specific; estimate it from the reference corpus | Synonym flattening makes prose dull; over-repetition | MTLD and HD-D (McCarthy & Jarvis 2010), not raw TTR; z-score against the reference interval |
| T4 | **Prefer plain, frequent words where the register allows**, especially evaluative adjectives and adverbs (*remarkable, seamless, comprehensive, notably, effectively*). | S; P | Reverses evaluative inflation. Evaluative adjectives and adverbs dominate the Liang lists. | Liang ICML 2024 (top-100 adjectives and adverbs); Kobak 2025 (14% of excess words are adjectives, 66% verbs) | — | Too colloquial for academic register | Rate of evaluative adjectives/adverbs per 1,000 words; word-frequency (Zipf) profile vs reference (heuristic; no literature effect size, see research §5) |
| T5 | **Unpack trailing present-participial clauses** (", highlighting …", ", ensuring …", ", underscoring …") into finite clauses with an explicit subject, a separate sentence, or deletion when the clause only restates. | Syn | The register feature with the largest instruction-tuned gap | Reinhart 2025 (GPT-4o 5.3×, d = 1.38; instruction-tuned 2–5×); Suvanto 2026 (participles more prominent in LLM fiction) | Close up to a 2–5× rate gap toward 1× | Choppier prose; dropped logical link | spaCy: count `advcl`/`acl` with a VBG head preceded by a comma, per 1,000 words, vs reference |
| T6 | **Denominalise**: turn abstract nouns back into verbs with agents ("the implementation of X enables the improvement of Y" → "implementing X improves Y" / "we implemented X, and Y improved"). | Syn; P | Reduces nominal density typical of LLM academic prose | Reinhart 2025 (2.1×, d = 1.23; instruction-tuned 1.5–2×); Herbold 2023 (more nominalisations) | Close a 1.5–2× gap | Changes agency (who did what): only name agents present in the source | Suffix-based nominalisation rate (-tion/-ment/-ness/-ity/-ance/-ence) per 1,000 words |
| T7 | **Thin phrasal coordination**: break automatic doublets and triplets ("clear and concise", "X, Y, and Z") unless each item carries content. | Syn; S | Reduces a reflexive list rhythm | Reinhart 2025 (phrasal coordination 1.9×, d = 0.81) | Close a ~1.9× gap | Loss of content if a list item was informative | Count coordinated ADJ/NOUN pairs and triples (dependency `conj` within NPs) per 1,000 words |
| T8 | **Restore human-typical passives and subject structures where the register uses them**: agentless passives in academic or method prose; avoid that-clauses as subject ("That X is Y is clear"). | Syn | LLMs under-use agentless passives and over-use that-clause subjects | Reinhart 2025 (agentless passive ~0.5×; that-clause subject 2.6×, d = 0.77) | Close ~0.5× and 2.6× gaps | Over-passivisation in registers that prefer active voice | Passive rate (`auxpass`/`nsubjpass`) and that-clause-subject count vs reference |
| T9 | **Break repeated syntactic templates**: vary clause order, sentence openings and POS skeletons across sentences. Do not open consecutive sentences with the same structure ("This X …", "By doing X, …"). | Syn; E | Templates are learned in pre-training and not removed by decoding settings | Shaib 2024 (95% of model outputs vs 38% of human references contain length-6 templates; 96.6–97.4% across temperatures); LAMP (POS templates such as DT NN IN NN CC are edited 27–54% of the time by writers) | Close a 95% vs 38% template prevalence gap | Unnatural variation for its own sake | POS 6-gram template rate and POS-sequence compression ratio (Shaib `diversity` package); sentence-opening POS bigram entropy |
| T10 | **Shorten dependencies, unstack nouns, keep relativisers** ("the method that we propose" rather than "the proposed method framework design"). | Syn; P | LLM syntax is harder to process: longer dependencies, stacked nouns, reduced relatives | Clark, Martin, Mahowald 2026; Muñoz-Ortiz 2024 (human dependency lengths more optimised; LLMs use more SBAR, fewer amod/appos) | — | Longer sentences if overdone | Mean dependency distance (spaCy); noun-compound chains ≥ 3; count of object relatives without a relativiser |
| T11 | **Vary sentence length according to content (burstiness)**: a short sentence for a key claim, longer ones for qualification. Do not insert fragments at random. | Syn; P | LLM sentence lengths cluster in a narrow band | Muñoz-Ortiz 2024 (models over-produce 10–30-token sentences); Perkins 2024 (higher-burstiness outputs detected 15.9%); Goh & Barabási 2008 (burstiness coefficient B) | Detector effect from Perkins (prompted burstiness, 7 detectors): 15.9% detection rate | Rhythm becomes mannered; fragments in formal register | Sentence-length SD, coefficient of variation, B = (σ − μ)/(σ + μ); compare with the reference interval |
| T12 | **Drop formulaic scaffolding**: restating conclusions, thesis-first signposting, theme explanation at the end. End on the last substantive point. | D | LLM essays and stories follow a fixed arc and over-explain | Herbold 2023 ("In conclusion" opens all ChatGPT conclusions); SlopShape 2026 (conclusions restate thesis); StoryScope 2026 (theme explained 77% vs 52%); Argument Collapse 2026 (argumentation fills 89.7% of paragraphs vs 71.5%) | Close a 77% vs 52% over-explanation rate (fiction) | Some genres require a summary (abstracts, reports): keep it when the register requires it | Embedding cosine between the final and opening paragraphs; detector for summary openers; paragraph-function tags via a small LLM judge (optional) |
| T13 | **Re-calibrate stance**: replace blanket hedges and generic balance with the author's actual position. Keep hedges on genuinely uncertain claims; use modal and epistemic markers where humans would. | D | LLM prose is less committed and less authorial, with hedged, generalised claims | Herbold 2023 (humans use more modal/epistemic constructions); Jiang & Hyland 2024 (students show more epistemic stance and authorial presence); Argument Collapse 2026 (strong stance 76.1% human vs 63.4% LLM) | Close a ~13-point gap in strong-stance share (essays) | **Changing the author's epistemic commitment.** Only apply with a stance stated by the user or present in the source. | Hedge/booster/self-mention counts per 1,000 words (Hyland-style lexicon) before vs after, compared with the reference; human spot check |
| T14 | **Add reader engagement where the register expects it**: questions, direct address, brief asides, in essays, blogs and opinion pieces, not in technical reports. | D | LLM essays use fewer engagement markers | Jiang & Hyland 2025 (fewer engagement markers, "particularly questions and personal asides"); StoryScope (humans address the reader 28% vs 7%) | Close the 28% vs 7% direct-address gap (fiction) | Register mismatch; fake intimacy | Count of questions, 2nd-person pronouns and parenthetical asides per 1,000 words vs reference |
| T15 | **Specificity from the source only**: replace vague allusions and generic examples with the concrete referent already in the user's notes or source (names, numbers, places, dates, titles). If none exists, ask the user; never invent one. | D; E | AI text prefers vague, generalised content | StoryScope 2026 (vague allusions 72% vs 50%; named references 47% vs 24%); Argument Collapse (hedged, generalised sub-arguments); Slop 2025 (density and relevance are the strongest slop predictors) | Close a 47% vs 24% named-reference gap (fiction) | **Fabrication**, the main risk. Enforce that every new entity or number is in the allowed-facts set. | Named-entity and number sets: after ⊆ (before ∪ user-supplied facts); count of concrete NEs per 100 words |
| T16 | **De-homogenise content**: keep the author's idiosyncratic arguments, examples and digressions; do not smooth them toward the consensus version. | D; E | LLM text converges on shared arguments and narrative choices | Argument Collapse (unique main arguments 65.3% human vs 3.4% LLM); Padmakumar & He 2024 (homogenisation 0.1660 with InstructGPT vs 0.1536 solo); Doshi & Hauser 2024; StoryScope (rarity 0.71 vs 0.49) | — | None if only preserving; inventing idiosyncrasy is fabrication | Embedding distance of the rewrite from a "typical answer" centroid (generate k = 5 small-model answers to the same prompt); must not decrease vs the source |
| T17 | **Fiction-specific (LAMP / TTCW / StoryScope)**: cut clichés and purple metaphors; name emotions plainly instead of bodily clichés ("heart hammered against ribs"); allow unresolved endings, subplots and moral ambiguity where the draft supports them; cut exposition that explains the scene. | D; S | Matches the edit categories expert writers apply and narrative features that separate human stories | LAMP 2025 (awkward word choice 28%, poor sentence structure 20%, unnecessary exposition 18%, clichés 17%); StoryScope (explicit emotion labels 29% human vs 8% AI); Antislop body-cliché trigrams; TTCW (LLM stories pass 9–30% of tests) | Writer-edited text preferred 65% of the time; LLM-edited > LLM-generated (LAMP) | Plot changes are content changes and need user consent | Antislop trigram hits; ratio of explicit emotion words to bodily-reaction phrases; LLM judges do **not** match experts on TTCW (Chakrabarty 2024), so use them only as weak signals |
| T18 | **Raise surprisal variance, not mean**: make word choices informative at content positions (precise verb, specific noun) while keeping function words predictable; avoid "safe" filler sentences. | P | Human text has higher UID variance, more tail-rank tokens and higher token-probability variance | GPT-who 2024 (human UID higher mean and SD); Holtzman 2020; GLTR (more out-of-top-100 tokens); Cheng 2025 (human PPL 15.02, AdvPara 14.26–17.11, simple paraphrase 9.28) | Bring PPL and the surprisal SD into the human interval. AuthorMist shows the overshoot failure (PPL 300–400 vs ~50–75 human). | Rare-word stuffing is a different artefact (Liang 2023 shows detectors respond to lexical sophistication) | Local LM (≤1.5B, e.g. Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B or GPT-2): token log-prob mean and SD, GLTR top-10/100/1000 bucket shares, Binoculars-style observer/performer ratio; z-scores vs reference |
| T19 | **Exemplar-conditioned rewriting**: give the rewriter 2–5 passages of the user's own prose (or a register-matched human text) and ask it to match their sentence-level habits, not their content. | E; Syn; S | Style embeddings are the signal that generic attacks leave unchanged; matching a specific human style moves it | Rivera Soto 2026 (StyleDetect AUROC ~95 under generic attacks; a style-aware paraphraser evades on single documents); CoPA 2025 (human-vs-machine contrast); Pedrotti 2025 (style-shifted DPO drops MAGE 76% → 47%) | Single-document: style detector defeated (Rivera Soto). Multi-document: detection recovers. | Copying exemplar content; privacy of user samples | Style-embedding cosine (an authorship-representation encoder, or a sentence encoder as proxy) to exemplars vs to the source; must move toward exemplars without content overlap (n-gram overlap with exemplars < threshold) |
| T20 | **Bounded passes with selection**: at most 2–3 edit passes; score each candidate with the CHECK battery and keep the best, instead of iterating until a detector passes. | P; E; D | Avoids attractor cycles, perplexity drift and quality loss | Wang 2025 (2-period attractor cycles); Sadasivan 2023 (each recursive round costs perplexity); TH-Bench (recursion PPL ~4× original and can *increase* detection) | — | Compute cost | Log every pass's metric vector; choose by distance-to-reference with a meaning-gate veto |

## C. Excluded: detector gaming (out of scope)

| Operation | Reported effect | Why excluded | Check-round action |
